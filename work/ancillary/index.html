The challenge

Make extra services easy to understand and purchase.

Guests reached this experience after booking, when they were already thinking about the holiday ahead. The business opportunity was to make relevant upgrades and complementary services easy to discover without turning the page into an overwhelming catalogue.

I started from a reference concept and developed a branded desktop and mobile proposal in Figma. The work covered the booking hero, information hierarchy, responsive grid and breakpoints, a reusable card system, interactive modal states, cart and checkout. Spacing, typography, colour and components were refined through design reviews with my UI/UX mentor and the wider digital team to preserve visual coherence with the brand kit and Design System.

Key interaction states

The interface reveals complexity only when it becomes useful.

01Discover

A scalable card system, organised without overload.

The service taxonomy covered food plans, beach equipment, activities, linen, family and dog-friendly extras. I translated it into category pills and a modular card system built on a consistent grid, so guests could scan one service group at a time.

Every card follows the same visual hierarchy—image ratio, benefit-led content, price and CTA—while selected states provide immediate feedback. The hero restores the booking context, and spacing, typography, colour and component states maintain visual coherence with the Club del Sole brand kit and Design System.

02Decide

Progressive detail at the moment of choice.

The cards stay deliberately concise. Opening a service reveals the extended description, a larger image, the calculated price and quantity controls only when the guest asks for more detail.

This progressive disclosure keeps the main page easy to scan while giving the modal enough information for a considered purchase. The primary action remains close to the controls and the updated total.

03Complete

A checkout that preserves context and confidence.

A persistent cart badge makes the selected extras available throughout the journey. In the summary, guests can review, edit or remove services and understand quantities, partial prices and the total before continuing.

The final confirmation closes the loop with a positive message and a complete recap. No new decision is introduced after payment: the interface simply reassures the guest that the stay has been updated successfully.

Responsive design

One journey, designed for two different contexts.

Mobile uses its own breakpoint, one-column grid and touch-target rules rather than compressing the desktop page. Spacing, typography and hierarchy support a vertical purchase path, while horizontal card rails preserve a legible card width and use the partial next card as a clear swipe affordance.
